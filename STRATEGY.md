# Strategy

The judge picks the summary "a reader who missed the day would most want to
read", weighing five criteria in order: it must be a summary of 11 November
2026 specifically; selection and breadth; specific and checkable; faithful
and neutral; tight and clear. The summary is built to win on each, in that
order.

1. **Dated to the day.** It opens "World news, Wednesday 11 November 2026"
   and leads with the two stories that only happened that day: day two of the
   Abu Dhabi talks and the COP31 leaders' segment opening. Events from
   Monday/Tuesday are dated as such ("on Tuesday", "on Monday"), and a big
   story from another day (Ronaldo's retirement, 5 November) was cut entirely
   rather than smuggled in undated.
2. **Breadth across regions and fields.** Diplomacy (Abu Dhabi), climate
   (COP31), US politics (Hegseth vote, midterm count), the Middle East (Iran,
   Hormuz), Asia (Bundang bus crash), Israel (Knesset), and tech/business
   (Anthropic IPO, Nvidia). No single country or beat dominates.
3. **Specific and checkable.** Nearly every sentence carries a figure
   (211-204, 254-163, 92 drones, 1.47C, 77 leaders, 37 ships, 57 injured,
   $70-78) and the outlet that reported it is named inline (BBC, Reuters,
   AP, AFP, WMO). The rubric names the outlet as part of checkability, so
   the attributions are part of the text, not a footnote. Unconfirmed claims
   are attributed ("Russia said...") instead of stated as fact.
4. **Neutral.** No adjectives of praise or blame, no speculation about what
   happens next, no editorialising. Quotes are short and attributed.
5. **Tight.** 1,969 characters: five short paragraphs ordered by importance,
   one or two sentences a story, then a dense "Also" line for the rest. Close
   to the 2,000-character cap because length buys breadth, but nothing is
   padded to fill it.
