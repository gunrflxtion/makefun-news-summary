World news, Wednesday 11 November 2026.

US-mediated Russia-Ukraine talks entered a second day in Abu Dhabi, after a four-hour first session President Volodymyr Zelenskyy called "constructive" (BBC). Moscow pressed for Ukraine to give up the rest of Donetsk; Kyiv demanded security guarantees and refused to cede land. No deal was reached (Reuters). Fighting continued: Russia said it shot down 92 drones over the Leningrad region, and a drone strike cut power in part of Kryvyi Rih (Reuters).

World leaders began speaking at the COP31 summit in Antalya, Turkey, where the World Meteorological Organization put January-to-September temperatures 1.47C above pre-industrial levels (AFP). Only 77 leaders were listed to speak, down from 116 at COP28; finance for poorer countries was the sharpest dispute.

The House voted 211-204 on Tuesday to send an impeachment resolution against War Secretary Pete Hegseth to committee, effectively killing it; the articles, from Republican Thomas Massie, accused him of waging the Iran war without Congress's approval (AP). Democrats won both chambers in last week's midterms (AP: 254-163 in the House, 18 uncalled).

The Justice Department charged three men on Tuesday it called Iranian operatives over an alleged plot against RAF Fairford in England (AFP). President Donald Trump said on Monday, after meeting Qatar's prime minister, that the war with Iran was "effectively over"; Tehran denied any talks. Tankers were still burning in the Strait of Hormuz after 37 ships were attacked in October, as oil prices fell to two-month lows (Reuters).

Also: a bus ploughed into 32 vehicles in Bundang, South Korea, injuring 57, nine seriously (Reuters); Israel's new Knesset was sworn in as Gadi Eisenkot began coalition talks with neither bloc holding a majority (BBC); and Anthropic launched its IPO roadshow at $70 to $78 a share as Nvidia's chief faced an FBI inquiry over an alleged affair with a suspected Chinese spy (Reuters).
